Most customers now begin with a question typed into a phone: "best caterer in Dar es Salaam", "pharmacy open now", "who builds websites in Tanzania". Increasingly they ask an assistant such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude, and take the answer it gives. If your business does not appear, the customer does not know you exist, however good you are.

Being found is not a trick. It is a set of ordinary things done carefully, in a sensible order. This checklist covers them, and what results to expect.

## First, understand how people find you

There are three places a customer meets your business before they ever call you:

1. **Google search and Google Maps,** where people look for a service near them.
2. **AI assistants,** which read many pages and then summarise the answer for the customer.
3. **Social media and word of mouth,** which lead people to search for your name.

All three look for the same thing: clear, accurate, consistent facts about who you are, what you do, where you are and how to reach you.

## 1. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

This is the listing that appears on the map and beside your name. It is free, and it is usually the fastest improvement.

- Claim it and verify it, so that it is yours.
- Use your exact business name, address, phone number and opening hours.
- Choose the right main category and add the services you offer.
- Add real photographs of your premises, your team and your work.
- Keep it current, especially holiday hours.

## 2. Give your website a clear structure

Your website is the place that search tools and assistants trust most, because you control it.

- **One page for each main service,** with its own heading and a few honest paragraphs, not everything crowded onto one page.
- **Your city and the areas you serve,** written in plain words.
- **A clear title and short description** for every page, so that it makes sense in a list of results.
- **Contact details on every page,** and a WhatsApp button that works.
- **Your own photographs,** with a short description for each, so that people using screen readers and search tools understand them.
- **English and Kiswahili,** if your customers use both. Each language needs to be written properly by someone who knows it well.

## 3. Make it fast, secure and mobile-friendly

Google measures how a site behaves on a phone, and so do your customers.

- The site should open quickly on a mobile connection and be easy to use with one thumb.
- It should use a secure connection on every page, shown by the padlock.
- It should be kept up to date and checked for damage. A site that has been hacked is flagged in results and visitors are warned away from it.

## 4. Tell the search engines you exist

Search engines find new sites on their own, but slowly. You can help them.

- Register the site with **Google Search Console** and **Bing Webmaster Tools.** Both are free.
- Submit your **sitemap,** the list of your pages.
- Use the reports to see which searches bring visitors and which pages have problems.

## 5. Answer the questions your customers really ask

Search is built on questions. Write short, useful pages and articles that answer the ones you hear every week: what does it cost, how long does it take, what do I need to bring, how does it work. Write them in your own voice, with real detail from your work in Tanzania. Assistants prefer a clear answer from a business that knows the subject.

## 6. Collect honest reviews

Reviews build trust with customers and strengthen your listing.

- Ask satisfied customers to leave a review, and make it easy with a direct link.
- Reply to every review politely, including the critical ones.
- Never buy reviews or write your own. Search engines and customers both notice.

## 7. Keep the facts the same everywhere

Your name, address, phone number and description should read the same on your website, your Google listing, your social media and any directory you appear in. When these disagree, both search engines and assistants lose confidence in them.

## 8. Be mentioned by others

A mention from a supplier, a partner, a chamber of commerce, a local directory or a news site tells search tools that your business is real. Ask for these naturally, through the people you already work with. Do not pay for lists of links. It can harm you.

## 9. Be easy for AI assistants to understand

Assistants answer by reading pages and picking out facts. Help them by writing plainly:

- A short paragraph on your home or About page that states who you are, what you do, where you work and how to contact you.
- Clear headings and short sentences, without slogans.
- Business details marked up in the code of the page, in the form that search tools read, so they do not have to guess.

## 10. Measure and keep going

Look at the figures every month: how many people visited, which pages they read, how many pressed call or WhatsApp. Use a method that respects visitors' privacy. Adjust what is not working, and add one useful page at a time.

## What honest results look like

Being found grows slowly. A new site often takes several weeks to appear properly, and real results usually take a few months of steady work. Be careful with anyone who promises first place on Google, or a fixed date for it. Nobody controls the order of the results, and a promise like that usually means something is being hidden.

## Questions to ask whoever helps you

1. Which of these steps are included, and which are not?
2. Will the accounts for Google, Bing and analytics be in my name?
3. What will you report to me each month?
4. How do you deal with a site that is hacked or goes offline?
5. Will the writing be in my own words, in both languages?
6. What can you not promise?

## A last word

A customer who needs what you offer is searching right now. Being found is how a good business reaches the people who are looking for it, and it rarely needs more than care, patience and honest information.

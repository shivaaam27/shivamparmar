Almost every business owner who contacts us asks the same first question: how much will it cost? It is a fair question. It is your money, and many owners have already paid for a website that arrived late, loaded slowly or was never finished.

The straight answer is that a professional website costs more than the cheapest offer you will find, and far less than the cost of getting it wrong. The exact figure depends on what your business needs, and a good developer will give it to you in writing before you pay anything. This guide explains what sits behind that figure, so you can compare quotes on the same terms.

## What decides the price

- **The amount of content.** The number of pages matters, but so does the writing, the photographs and any video that must be prepared.
- **Languages.** A site in English and Kiswahili needs every page written, designed and checked twice.
- **Features.** A simple presentation of your business is one project. Online bookings, ordering, customer accounts or payment by mobile money and card are another.
- **Connections to the tools you already use.** Accounting, stock, email and WhatsApp can all be linked, and each link takes careful work.
- **Design.** A design made for your brand takes more time than a ready-made template, and it shows.
- **Timing.** Work that must be finished in days needs to be planned differently from work that has weeks.

## Why two quotes can be far apart

Two quotes for the same number of pages can describe very different work. A low quote usually leaves things out, and they return later as extra charges or as problems. When you compare, compare what is included, not only the total.

## What a professional website should include

- **A design made for the phone first.** Most of your visitors will arrive on a phone, often on a slow or costly connection, so the site must load quickly and be easy to use with one thumb.
- **Clear writing.** Within seconds a visitor should know what you do, who it is for, where you are and how to reach you.
- **Your own photographs** wherever possible. Real pictures of your business build more trust than stock images.
- **Set-up for search.** Proper page titles and descriptions, a map listing, and registration with Google and Bing, so that people searching for a business like yours can find you.
- **Contact that works.** A WhatsApp button, a phone number, a form and an email address, all tested.
- **Visitor figures** that respect your visitors' privacy, so you know what is working.

## Security: the part that is rarely explained

A website is a door to your business. Ask who locks it, and who keeps checking.

- **A secure connection** on every page, shown by the padlock in the browser, renewed automatically before it expires.
- **Careful handling of customer information.** Collect only what you need, store it safely, and follow the data protection rules that apply in Tanzania.
- **Private, strong logins** for everyone who manages the site, with a second step to sign in for the administrator. Passwords are never sent around in chat groups.
- **Regular updates.** Most small websites that are hacked were simply out of date.
- **Backups kept separately and tested**, so that if something goes wrong the site can be restored quickly.
- **Protection against spam and automated attacks** on forms and sign-in pages.
- **Payments through approved providers.** Card details should never be stored on your own site.
- **Ownership in your name.** The domain and the hosting account should belong to you, not to the person who built the site.

## What good delivery looks like

- **A written scope and a fixed price** before any work begins, so both sides know what is being delivered.
- **A timeline with dates** for the design, your feedback, the build, the testing and the launch. A business website usually takes a few weeks, not a few days. Larger systems take longer.
- **Progress you can see.** You review the work in stages and are not shown it only at the end.
- **Testing on real phones** before launch, not only on a computer.
- **A proper launch.** The domain connected, business email working, search tools set up, and everything checked once more.
- **A handover.** You receive the logins, a short guide and time to ask questions.
- **Support after launch.** You know whom to call if something stops working, and how quickly they will reply.

## Costs that continue after launch

A website has running costs, and you should know them before you start: the domain name each year, the hosting, business email, and the maintenance that keeps the site secure and up to date. Ask for these in writing, with the dates they fall due, so there are no surprises later.

## Questions to ask before you pay

1. What exactly is included, and what is not?
2. Who will own the domain, the hosting account and the content?
3. What happens if I later want to work with someone else?
4. How is the site secured, and how are backups handled?
5. Can I see sites you have built and speak to the owners?
6. Who writes the text and takes the photographs?
7. When will it be ready, and what happens if the date is missed?
8. How many rounds of changes are included?
9. What will I pay every year to keep it running?
10. Who do I call when I need help?
